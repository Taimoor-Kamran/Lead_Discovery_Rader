- **Author:** Taimoor

---

## Goal

Add a per-domain evidence axis built from DNS and RDAP that does not depend on fetching the
homepage, so a business whose site answers with a bot challenge, a non-2xx page, or nothing
at all still produces true, citable findings.

Today 21 of the 147 production businesses sit at `bot_challenge`, `not_readable` or
`unreachable` and emit zero or near-zero findings. DNS and RDAP answer for all of them.

Two rules govern everything below:

1. **Certainty or silence.** A DNS answer of `NOERROR` with no record of that type means the
   record is genuinely absent → emit. `SERVFAIL`, `REFUSED`, timeout, or any transport error
   means unknown → store `null`, emit nothing. A failure that reads like an answer is the same
   defect shape as the Cloudflare pages in v0.12.0.
2. **No people.** Business-level registry facts only. Registrant name, email, phone and
   postal address are dropped at the parse boundary and never stored or logged. No real
   person's data is ever fixtured.

---

## Scope

- `core/dns.py` — resolver behind an injectable interface, plus a fixture-backed resolver.
- `core/rdap.py` — RDAP over HTTPS, rate-limited and daily-capped like Places and PSI.
- `domain_intel` cache table keyed by registrable domain, plus an Alembic migration.
- The full snapshot copied into the audit's `checks` so findings stay reproducible from the
  audit row alone.
- Nine new finding codes with `CATALOGUE` entries.
- Per-status allow-list changes so domain findings survive `bot_challenge`, `not_readable`
  and `unreachable`.
- New settings, all with defaults.
- Tests with fixtures recorded from real domains among the 147, plus one live smoke script.

## Out of scope

- DKIM — selectors are not discoverable, so absence can never be proven.
- Parked-domain inference.
- SPF recursive lookup counting (the 10-lookup limit).
- DNSSEC validation, RBL/blacklist checks, IP geolocation, reverse DNS, zone transfer.
- Port-43 WHOIS. DNS-over-HTTPS.
- Raising `AUDIT_LOGIC_VERSION` to 9 — see **Blockers**.
- No frontend work beyond the nine label entries. The new findings render through the
  existing `FindingList`.
- Any registrant or personal contact data, in any form, for any reason.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A DNS query answering `NOERROR` with no record of that type produces the finding. The same
   query answering `SERVFAIL`, `REFUSED`, timing out, or `NXDOMAIN` on the apex produces
   `null` and **no** finding. Proven by one test per response code.
2. The resolver actually used is recorded in the stored snapshot.
3. An RDAP fixture containing registrant name, email, phone and address results in a stored
   `domain_intel` row and a stored audit `checks` blob containing none of those four values.
   Asserted against the stored row, not against the parser's return value.
4. A business whose latest audit status is `bot_challenge` still receives its domain findings.
   On that status only `domain_expired`, `domain_no_a_record` and `domain_expiring_soon` can
   support an opportunity; no other finding code contributes, and a `bot_challenge` business
   with no domain findings still produces no opportunity.
5. A business on a builder subdomain, and a business whose only URL is a social profile,
   produce zero DNS queries and zero RDAP calls.
6. `domain_no_a_record` never appears on an audit that fetched a 2xx page.
7. Every one of the nine finding codes has a fixture, chosen in this order of preference:
   first a real recorded response from a domain among the 147; failing that, a real recorded
   response from any real domain that shows the condition; failing that, a hand-built fixture
   from a real response shape, with `synthetic` in its filename. The fixture fails against the
   code as it stands before this spec.
8. Running the audit twice within `DNS_INTEL_TTL_DAYS` issues DNS queries only on the first run.
9. With `DNS_ENABLED=false` and `RDAP_ENABLED=false`, the full existing test suite passes
   unchanged.
10. `AUDIT_LOGIC_VERSION` is still 8 at merge.

## Implementation notes

### Finding codes

| Code | Severity | Service | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| `domain_expired` | high | `website_design` | RDAP expiry date is in the past |
| `domain_no_a_record` | high | `website_design` | site host has no A, no AAAA and no CNAME, all `absent`, and no 2xx page in this audit |
| `domain_expiring_soon` | medium | `website_design` | expiry within `AUDIT_DOMAIN_EXPIRY_WARN_DAYS` |
| `multiple_spf_records` | medium | `null` | more than one apex TXT beginning `v=spf1` |
| `spf_allows_all` | medium | `null` | SPF `all` mechanism qualified `+` or unqualified |
| `no_spf` | low | `null` | apex TXT `absent` of any `v=spf1` record |
| `no_dmarc` | low | `null` | `_dmarc` TXT `absent` of any `v=DMARC1` record |
| `dmarc_policy_none` | low | `null` | DMARC record present with `p=none` |
| `no_domain_mx` | low | `null` | apex MX `absent` |

Only the three `website_design` codes map to something FlexTBS sells. The six email codes
carry `service: null` on purpose: they are context for the rep, they create no opportunity,
and they never reach the CRM as a claim FlexTBS cannot service. If the client later says
otherwise, that is a new spec.

Nothing above `medium` unless the answer is certain, and nothing above `high` at all.

### Wording

Messages state the registry or DNS fact and stop. No inference about the business.
`domain_expired` reads as *"Registry records show the domain registration expired on <date>."*
— not *"the domain is expired"*, which would be a claim about a state we cannot see.

### Provider mapping

NS and MX hosts are mapped to a provider name (builder, registrar, Google Workspace,
Microsoft 365) and stored as facts in the snapshot. No finding, no inference, no opportunity.

### Sequencing

Domain intelligence runs after the fetch outcome is known, because `domain_no_a_record` needs
the contradiction guard and the site host comes from `final_url`. It runs regardless of whether
the fetch succeeded.

- **C2. Wording rule.** `findings.py:74` `ALLOWED_OPENINGS = ("Audit found", "Audit could not",

  `registrar` whose name is in its `vcardArray` `fn`. "Keep registrar name" and "drop every
  `entities` / vCard object at parse time" contradict. Proposal: at the parse boundary, read
  only `fn` from the entity whose roles include `registrar` (an organisation), then discard

  citing `no_spf` under `website_design` would pass and could reach the CRM. Proposal: the
  guardrail rejects evidence whose finding's catalogue service is `null` or differs from the

- **C5. The CRM payload carries every finding.** `crm/payload.py:251–256` `top_findings` →

  and `spf_allows_all` (medium) would land in the CRM `top_findings` field. Proposal: exclude

- **C6. "No frontend work" vs label coverage.** `tests/unit/test_label_coverage.py:92` requires

  `make check` to pass. Proposal: data-only label entries, no component changes.
- **C7. "Never fixtured" vs "a fixture that contains registrant contact data".** Goal rule 2 and
  Task 8 contradict. Proposal: that one fixture takes a real RDAP response's *shape* with every
  contact value replaced by an obviously fake one (`example.invalid`, `+1 555 0100`, a
  made-up name and street), so no real person's data is committed.
- **C8. DMARC and TXT records can carry personal data.** DMARC `rua=`/`ruf=` are `mailto:`
  addresses — often a person's mailbox — and apex TXT can hold anything. Storing "the actual
  record" as evidence (Task 5) would store them. Proposal: store only `v=spf1` TXT values and
  a count of the rest; store the DMARC record with every `mailto:` address replaced by
  `[address removed]`; never quote other TXT records.
- **C9. `NXDOMAIN` on `_dmarc.<apex>` and on a `www` host.** A domain with no DMARC record
  normally answers `NXDOMAIN` for `_dmarc.<apex>`, not `NOERROR`-empty. If only `NOERROR`-empty

  (not on `REFUSED` / `NXDOMAIN`); the retry uses what is left of the one 5-second budget.
  TCP is used when the UDP answer is truncated.
- **Budget (C15).** DNS questions run concurrently and DNS runs beside RDAP; the step waits at
  most 15 s. Anything unfinished is `TIMEOUT` / `unknown` and the reason is recorded under
  `reasons`. RDAP has one attempt, 5 s connect + 10 s read.
- **Cache rules.** A DNS snapshot is cached only when at least one question answered
  `NOERROR`; an all-`unknown` snapshot is used by that audit but not stored. A cached DNS
  snapshot is reused only for the same site host. An RDAP error, a missing expiry date, or no
  endpoint is never cached, so it is asked again next audit. The upsert is
  `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE`, so two audits of one domain cannot collide.

  migration tests that assert the exact set of tables a downgrade removes now include it.

- **Migration renumbered.** `0014_domain_intel` is now `0015_domain_intel`, with
  `down_revision = "0014_opportunity_withdrawal"`. Its content is unchanged and it is still
  the migration Taimoor approved; it has still not been run on dev or production. The

  `withdrawn_at` / `withdrawn_reason`) as they were; down to `0013_not_readable_status`

- **Amendment W — withdrawal judges each kind of evidence by its own lookup.**
  *The gap:* v0.12.1 lets only a `done` or `skipped` audit withdraw anything (its
  correction H), because an audit that could not read the page has no page findings for
  a reason that says nothing about the business. Under this spec a `bot_challenge`,
  `not_readable` or `unreachable` audit can carry domain findings and (Task 6 / C1)
  support a `website_design` opportunity from them. Such an opportunity could then never
  be withdrawn, even when a later audit's DNS and RDAP lookups worked and showed the
  domain finding gone — the orphan problem v0.12.1 exists to fix. The opposite gap is
  just as real: a `done` audit whose DNS or RDAP lookup failed would withdraw a row
  resting on `domain_expired` because the finding is missing, though nothing was learnt.

  *The rule:* one audit, two kinds of evidence, each judged on whether that lookup
  actually worked.
  - Page evidence — any cited finding that is not one of the nine domain codes, or no
    cited finding at all (`ads_social`) — is judged only by an audit that read the page
    (`done` / `skipped`), exactly as in v0.12.1.
  - Domain evidence is judged only when the audit's stored `checks.domain_intel`
    snapshot answered the question behind **every** cited domain finding, whatever the
    homepage status: RDAP facts present for `domain_expired` / `domain_expiring_soon`;
    the site host's `A`, `AAAA` and `CNAME` not `unknown` for `domain_no_a_record`; apex
    `TXT` for the SPF codes; `_dmarc` `TXT` for the DMARC codes; apex `MX` for
    `no_domain_mx`. A skipped lookup (`skipped`, `robots_blocked`, `failed`, deny list,
    both halves off) answered nothing.
  - So withdrawal may run on any status when the lookups worked, but on an audit that did
    not read the page it may withdraw **only** rows whose cited findings are all domain
    findings. A `bot_challenge` audit still cannot withdraw a page-derived row, or a row
    citing both kinds, because it still could not look at the page. On such an audit the
    row is judged by its cited findings only (the merged result says nothing about the
    page's services there).
  - A permanently closed business is still withdrawn whatever its audit says.
  - The v0.12.1 backfill and `scripts/repair_wrongly_withdrawn.py` use the same per-row
    judgement (`opportunities.service.can_judge`), so the repair script never "restores"
    a row a working domain lookup rightly withdrew.

  *Code:* `domain_intel/evidence.py` (`DOMAIN_FINDINGS`, `lookup_worked`);
  `opportunities/service.py` (`can_judge`, `withdraw_unsupported`, and `classify` now
  also runs withdrawal on `bot_challenge` / `robots_blocked`). No stored data changes:
  no domain finding code exists in any stored audit yet.
  *Tests:* `tests/integration/test_withdrawal_domain.py` — both ways: a working lookup
  withdraws a domain-only row on `bot_challenge`, `not_readable`, `unreachable`, `failed`
  and `done`; a `bot_challenge` audit with a working lookup still keeps a page-only and a
  mixed row; a SERVFAIL on the question behind each DNS code, a failed RDAP call, or no
  snapshot at all withdraws nothing, on `bot_challenge` and on `done`; a domain finding
  still present keeps its row; the backfill agrees. The v0.12.1 tests in
  `test_withdrawal.py` (nothing withdrawn on an audit that could not look, closed
  business withdrawn) pass unchanged.
