# v0.12.0 — Finish the sentence

Status: draft
Branch: `spec/v0.12.0-finish-the-sentence`
Previous: v0.11.1 (safety pass), merged and tagged.
Input: `specs/audit-2026-09-25.md` — a line-cited audit of what the checks actually do.

## Goal

The client's verdict on two verified leads was "accurate but shallow". The audit shows why,
and it is not what either of us assumed. The audit does not lack checks. Ten of its checks
collect rich data and then emit a binary.

- `title_length` is stored. `missing_title` fires only when there is no title at all, so
  Ultimate Plumber's `Home | Business` passes clean (checks.py:262–267, findings.py:410).
- The viewport tag's full content string is stored. Only a missing tag is flagged, so
  `user-scalable=no` is never seen (checks.py:257–261, findings.py:408).
- Nine social platforms and their first href are stored. Nothing reads them
  (checks.py:412–431).
- The `<meta name="generator">` string, version and all, is stored. **No rule anywhere reads
  `tech_stack`** (checks.py:472–493).
- `builder_subdomain` fires only on a builder *subdomain*. Ultimate Plumber is Wix on its own
  domain and emits nothing (findings.py:374–382).
- LCP, CLS and TBT are collected and appear only inside another finding's evidence string
  (psi.py:110–113, findings.py:566–580).

Two of these are not shallow. They are wrong, in a document a rep reads out loud to a
prospect. A site carrying LocalBusiness as microdata is told it has none. A site whose JSON-LD
has a trailing comma is told the same, and the parse error is swallowed. `© 2018-2035` is
reported as a stale 2018.

This spec makes the checks finish their sentences. Almost every item works on data the audit
already has in hand, so the depth is close to free — and nothing here makes an outbound
request to anything other than the prospect's own homepage.

Budget: about five and a half days.

## Scope

### Part one — findings that are currently wrong

#### 1. Structured data must stop collapsing four states into one

`_structured_data` (checks.py:434–453) answers exactly one question: does some JSON-LD node
carry a LocalBusiness-family `@type`. Four materially different pages all return `False` and
receive the same low-severity `no_structured_data`:

| The page carries | Today | Should be |
|---|---|---|
| No structured data at all | `no_structured_data` | unchanged |
| Only `Organization` or `WebSite` | `no_structured_data` | a distinct finding — schema exists but not the local one |
| LocalBusiness JSON-LD that fails to parse | `no_structured_data` | a distinct finding — **broken markup**, higher severity |
| LocalBusiness as **microdata** (`itemtype`) | `no_structured_data` | **no finding** — the business has it |

The microdata case is a false statement about the prospect. The invalid-JSON case is silently
swallowed by `except (ValueError, TypeError): continue`, and it is the most sellable of the
four — "your structured data is there but broken" beats "you have none".

- Read microdata (`itemtype` / `itemscope`) as well as JSON-LD.
- Report a parse failure as its own finding, with the offending block as evidence. Do not
  swallow it.
- Distinguish "schema present, but no LocalBusiness" from "no schema".
- **Extract the fields** once a LocalBusiness node is found: `name`, `address`, `telephone`,
  `sameAs`, `openingHours`. Store them. Item 8 consumes them.
- Normalise the stored `@type` — today it keeps the raw string, so
  `https://schema.org/Plumber` and `Plumber` are different values.

#### 2. The copyright check discards the years that matter

`_copyright_year` (checks.py:509) keeps only years up to the current one. Two consequences,
both confirmed by running the function:

- **`© 2035` produces nothing.** The future year is discarded, the page then reads as having
  no copyright notice at all, and no finding fires. This is the exact string the client used
  as their headline example of what we miss.
- **`© 2018-2035` is reported as a stale 2018.** The range's later year is rejected, and the
  single-year pattern then picks up the earlier one.

- A year in the future is a finding in its own right — a template placeholder, not a stale
  site. It is more sellable than a stale year because it is visibly absurd.
- Parse ranges. `© 2018-2026` on a site in 2026 is current, not stale.
- Keep `stale_copyright` as it is for genuinely old years.

#### 3. Service mapping exists twice and the two disagree

A finding carries `service_category`, and `opportunities/catalogue.py` holds a separate
finding-to-service table. The audit found cases where they disagree. Every opportunity the
client sees is filed under one of these, and F1 in their queue brief ranks by service value —
so this has to be one source of truth before anything is built on top of it.

- Pick one as authoritative, derive the other from it, and delete the duplicate.
- Add a test that fails if any finding code maps to two different services.
- Report which findings were mis-filed and how many opportunities in the production database
  change service as a result.

### Part two — data collected but never judged

Each of these is a rule over a value the audit already stores. No new fetching.

#### 4. Evaluate the page title

`title` and `title_length` are both stored and neither is evaluated.

- Default and template titles: `Home | Business`, `Home`, `Untitled`, `Site`, and the common
  builder defaults. Treat "title contains no word from the business name" as the general case
  rather than maintaining a blocklist alone.
- Too short (under ~20 characters) and too long (over ~60, truncated in results).
- Evidence is the literal title string. The screenshot writes itself.

#### 5. Parse the viewport content

The full content string is stored. Flag `user-scalable=no` and `maximum-scale` below 1.5 —
ATX's zoom restriction, and an accessibility issue as well as a mobile one.

#### 6. A builder on the business's own domain

`builder_subdomain` covers `something.wixsite.com`. It misses the far more common case: a
Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy or Duda site on the business's own domain, which is what Ultimate
Plumber is. The platform label is already detected (fingerprints.py:140–158).

- Emit a finding for the detected builder regardless of domain, at a lower severity than the
  subdomain case. It maps to `website_design`.
- Nothing in the codebase currently reads `tech_stack`. Wire it into the rules.

#### 7. More than one H1

`h1_present` is a bool, but every heading level is already in `heading_structure.levels`.
Multiple H1s is a real SEO finding and the data is sitting there.

#### 8. Placeholder contact details, and a name/phone diff

The audit detects *that* a mailto exists but not *what* it points at, and never compares
what the site says against the Places record.

- Store the mailto address, not just its presence. Flag placeholder domains:
  `mysite.com`, `example.com`, `yourdomain`, `email.com`, `domain.com`. Ultimate Plumber's
  `info@mysite.com` means every emailed enquiry is lost — the single most sellable finding in
  either verification report.
- Flag placeholder footer text: `Business Name`, `Your Company`, `Company Name`, `Lorem ipsum`.
- Compare the site's `tel:` link against the Places `nationalPhoneNumber`, and the
  LocalBusiness `address` from item 1 against the Places address. A mismatch is a NAP finding
  — this is ATX's Suite #11 vs Ste 303.
- Compare the site's canonical URL against the Places `websiteUri`. Flag a stale `http://`
  or a non-www/www mismatch in the listing.

Normalise both sides before comparing, and reuse the existing normalisation rather than
writing a second one. A false NAP finding is worse than no NAP finding: say in the notes how
you avoided one.

#### 9. Click-to-call on its own

`no_contact_on_homepage` fires only when `tel:`, `mailto:` and a contact form are *all*
missing (findings.py:419). A site with a form and no phone link — normal on a builder
template, and a real loss for a home-services business — gets nothing. Emit a finding for a
missing `tel:` link independently.

### Part three — structural

#### 10. `method` on every finding

The finding model has `code`, `severity`, `service_category`, `message`, `evidence_text`,
`evidence_url`. Add `method`: `deterministic` | `api` | `ai`. Every check already knows which
it is. The client's brief asks for it, the queue needs it to show an AI finding differently
from a measured one, and it is the precondition for their F8 trust work.

#### 11. Re-audit when the check logic changes, not when the audit ages

Everything above changes what a check concludes about a page. Existing audits are now wrong,
and `needs_audit` treats any successful audit inside `AUDIT_MAX_AGE_DAYS` as fresh — so
without this, the client sees none of the new findings for thirty days.

- Add an `audit_logic_version` constant, stored on each audit.
- A business whose latest audit carries an older logic version is due immediately, regardless
  of age.
- Bump it in this spec, and document bumping it as part of shipping any check change.

This pays for itself on every spec after this one.

## Out of scope

- **Crawling beyond the homepage** (M1). It multiplies audit time by roughly ten and forces
  audit concurrency and tiering with it. Its own spec.
- **Response headers and the security family** (M6). The fetch result keeps no response
  headers, which is what makes mixed content, HSTS and security headers impossible. Retaining
  them is small; the checks on top of them are not. Next spec.
- **Social link validation.** The hrefs are stored and Ultimate Plumber's broken Facebook link
  is real, but checking it means an outbound request to a social platform. The project's
  constraint bars social scraping; the client's own brief allows "validating that the link
  resolves, nothing deeper". A `HEAD` that reads no content probably clears both. It needs a
  written decision and respx fixtures, not a quiet inclusion here.
- **Per-metric Core Web Vitals findings.** LCP, CLS and TBT deserve their own thresholds
  rather than living in another finding's evidence text, and INP is absent. Next spec.
- **Generator version parsing** ("outdated WordPress"). Cheap, but it needs a version
  database to be worth anything.
- **DNS, WHOIS/RDAP, SPF/DMARC** (M8). A whole axis, and a good one. Its own spec.
- **`dollar_band` and value ranking** (F1). Blocked on the client's price list.
- The two dead checks, `ecommerce` (checks.py:398–409) and `favicon` (checks.py:278–284),
  which collect data no rule reads. Decide in this spec whether to emit or delete them; do not
  build on them.

## Implementation notes

_(append only)_

**Why this order.** Part one is three findings that state something false about a prospect.
Depth on top of a wrong answer makes the wrong answer louder. Part two is where the value is
and it is nearly free — the data is already collected on every audit today and thrown away.
Part three is the plumbing that makes the next spec possible and stops this one from being
invisible for thirty days.

**On false positives.** Items 8 and 1 both compare the site against the Places record. Every
comparison is a chance to accuse a prospect of something untrue, in front of a rep who will
repeat it. Normalise hard, prefer `unknown` to a guess, and where a comparison is uncertain
say so in the evidence rather than raising a finding.

**Nothing here fetches anything new.** Every item works on the prospect's own homepage, which
is already fetched once per audit, or on the Places record already in hand. No new API calls,
no new cost, no new rate limit. That is deliberate — it keeps the spec's risk in the logic,
where tests can catch it.
