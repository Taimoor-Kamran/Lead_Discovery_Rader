# v0.11.1 — Safe to hand over

Status: draft
Branch: `spec/v0.11.1-safety-pass`
Previous: v0.11.0 (deeper audit), merged and tagged.

## Goal

v0.11.0 works. This spec is the short list of things that should not be in a build
somebody else runs.

Three items. None of them is a feature. Two are defects that lose data or hide failures
without saying so, and one is a licence obligation. Nothing here changes what the product
does — it makes what it already does trustworthy enough to hand to a client who will run
it without us watching.

Budget: about three days. If an item grows past that, it belongs in v0.12.0, not here.

## Scope

### 1. A failed audit must not hide a business for 30 days

`needs_audit` (`audit_web/service.py`) treats a business as fresh if it has *any* audit
inside `AUDIT_MAX_AGE_DAYS`, regardless of that audit's status. So one transient failure —
a timeout, a 5xx, a site that was briefly down — removes the business from every
subsequent run for a month, and nothing anywhere says so.

This is not theoretical. Two businesses are in that state right now, both from the RQ
timeout bug fixed in v0.11.0: **Hoffman Electric Company** and **Harlow Beauty and Hair
Salon**. Each carries a false `failed` audit as its latest, has zero opportunities, and
was skipped by the full overnight re-audit.

- A **failed** audit is due again after `AUDIT_FAILED_RETRY_HOURS` (default 6), not
  `AUDIT_MAX_AGE_DAYS`.
- A **successful** audit keeps the 30-day window unchanged.
- An **unreachable** site is a third case and needs its own decision: a site that is
  genuinely gone should not be retried every six hours forever. Propose a backoff, or a
  separate longer window, and say which you chose and why.
- Both existing damaged businesses become due immediately once this ships. Confirm they
  do, and that they then produce opportunities.

This is the highest-value item in the spec. Every lead it recovers is one the client paid
to discover and never saw.

### 2. Find out why one AI call in three fails

The Health page shows `openai: 35% (12 of 34 calls)` over 24 hours. All twelve were
`APIConnectionError: Connection error` — a connection failure, not auth, quota or rate
limiting. They began at 01:05:55 on 2026-09-25, seventy seconds before the host machine
slept, and continued for thirteen seconds after it woke.

The sleep is a plausible cause and is unconfirmed. It does not explain the seventy seconds
before it.

- Establish the cause. If it is the machine sleeping, prove it and record it — a laptop
  artefact is an acceptable answer, but only once it is demonstrated rather than assumed.
- If it is not the sleep, fix it.
- Either way, **a failed classification must be visible on the lead**. Today a business
  with no AI summary looks identical to one whose AI call failed. A reviewer cannot tell
  the difference, and neither could we.
- Add a retry with backoff for connection-class errors specifically, if the cause turns
  out to be transient. Do not retry auth or quota errors.

**If the cause cannot be established inside this spec's budget**, the fallback is to set
`AI_PROVIDER=disabled` in production and say so plainly. Shipping a feature that fails a
third of the time, silently, to somebody else's machine is worse than shipping without it.

### 3. "Powered by Google" attribution

Google's Maps Platform terms require attribution wherever Places data is displayed. The
product shows business names, phone numbers, addresses, ratings and review counts from
Places on the review queue, the lead detail page and the leads list, and exports them to
the CRM.

- Add the required attribution to every screen that displays Places-derived data.
- Follow Google's current attribution guidance for the wording and placement rather than
  inventing a form of words — check the documentation, do not reason from memory.
- Decide and record whether the CRM export also needs it. It carries Places data out of
  the product, which is the case the terms are concerned with.

This is a licence obligation on a product about to be run by a third party, not a polish
item.

## Implementation notes

_(append only)_

**Why these three and nothing else.** This build is about to be run by somebody who cannot
read the logs, cannot diagnose a stuck run, and will not know that a business quietly
vanished. Each item here is a case where the product fails without telling anyone: a lead
disappears for a month, an AI summary is missing with no explanation, or a licence term is
breached invisibly. Features can wait. Silence cannot.

**Item 1 — the retry window (implemented in `audit_web/service.py::needs_audit`).** The
wait before a business is due again now depends on its newest audit's status:

- `failed` → `AUDIT_FAILED_RETRY_HOURS` (6). A failed audit is a fault on our side (the
  RQ timeout of v0.11.0, an exception) and says nothing about the site.
- `unreachable` → a backoff, `AUDIT_UNREACHABLE_BACKOFF_DAYS` = 1, 3, 7: one step per
  *consecutive* unreachable audit, counted from the audits already stored (no migration),
  then the normal `AUDIT_MAX_AGE_DAYS`. A `done` audit resets the streak; a `failed` one
  ends it too (it is not evidence about the site).
- `done`, `skipped`, `robots_blocked` → `AUDIT_MAX_AGE_DAYS` (30), unchanged.

*Why a backoff for unreachable, and why these steps.* `unreachable` is broader than "the
site is gone": it is recorded for a connection that never opened, a 5xx, a homepage
fetch that failed, and a URL the SSRF guard refused (`service.py`). A flat 30 days is
wrong for the transient cases — and worse than hiding the business, an unreachable audit
*creates* a "Homepage could not be loaded" opportunity, so a site that was down for ten
minutes carries a false "your site is down" claim for a month. A flat 6 hours is wrong for
a site that is really gone. The backoff looks again the next day (before a reviewer is
likely to act on the claim), then at 3 and 7 days, then settles into the monthly rhythm:
a dead site costs three extra homepage fetches in total, ever. The refused-URL case is
deterministic and gains nothing from a retry, but its cost is the same three no-op checks
(the guard refuses before any network request), so it is not special-cased — one rule is
easier to reason about than two.

Hoffman Electric Company and Harlow Beauty and Hair Salon: this spec records that each
one's newest audit is a false `failed` one from before the v0.11.0 RQ-timeout fix, so it is
more than 6 hours old and `needs_audit` is true for both under the new rule (covered by `test_a_failed_audit_after_a_good_one_is_still_due_after_the_retry_window`).
Confirming it against the production database was not possible from this session — the
read was refused by the session's permission policy (production data) — so the acceptance
box is left to the human walk-through (Test plan steps 1–2).

**Item 2 — the OpenAI failures: diagnosis (the report asked for before fixing).**
Evidence gathered: the Windows System event log of the host for 2026-09-25 (read with
`Get-WinEvent`; the host clock is Pakistan Standard Time, UTC+5). The production
container logs and `ai_classifications` rows could not be read from this session (refused
as production data), so the failure timestamps used are the ones this spec records.

| UTC | Host event (System log) |
|---|---|
| 01:05:47 | Kernel-Power 506 "The system is entering Modern Standby"; 105 power source change at :50 |
| **01:05:55** | **first `APIConnectionError`** (per this spec) |
| 01:06:00 | Kernel-Power 172 "Connectivity state in standby: Disconnected, Reason: Adaptive Connected Standby" |
| 01:06:39–01:06:56 | brief standby exits/re-entries, connectivity flapping Connected/Disconnected |
| 01:07:07 | Kernel-Power 172 "Disconnected, Reason: Policy Setting" — the network is off for the night (this is the "sleep" the spec measured its 70 s against: 01:05:55 + 72 s) |
| 03:08:50 | Kernel-Power 507 "exiting Modern Standby" (wake); Wi-Fi adapter back from D3 |
| ~03:09:03 | last failure: "thirteen seconds after it woke" (per this spec) |
| 03:09:17 | Netwtw10 7021 — first Wi-Fi connection telemetry after wake |

Conclusion: **the failures are the laptop going into Modern Standby, not a product
fault.** The "seventy seconds before sleep" is explained: the machine entered Modern
Standby 8 s *before* the first failure, and Windows' Adaptive Connected Standby took the
network away at 01:06:00, a minute before the final policy disconnect that looks like
"sleep". The WSL VM (and so the worker) keeps running for a while in Modern Standby,
so calls continued into a dead network; after wake they failed until the Wi-Fi
re-associated ~27 s later. Every failure sits inside a window where the host had no
network. Nothing in the code or the provider was wrong. A human cross-check, if wanted:
the last failed call's `created_at` should be before 03:09:17 UTC.

Each of the 12 failures was already three attempts: the OpenAI SDK retries a connection
error twice (`AI_MAX_RETRIES=2`) with sub-second-to-few-second backoff. That cannot bridge
a ~27 s Wi-Fi reconnect, so the retry the spec allows for a transient cause was added
*on top*, for connection failures only: `AI_CONNECTION_RETRY_DELAYS_SECONDS` = 5, 10, 20
(35 s in all, covering the observed reconnect with margin). Deliberately **not** retried:
`APITimeoutError` (a subclass of `APIConnectionError` — the request may have reached
OpenAI and been billed, and the SDK has already retried it), and every status error
(401/403 auth, 429 quota, 4xx). One guard the spec did not ask for but the retry needs:
once a call has spent every pause and still cannot connect, the client stops pausing
until a call connects again. Without it, a classification run with the network gone
would spend ~40 s per business and a 50-business run would exceed `JOB_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`
(1800) and fail as a timeout — the v0.11.0 failure mode again. Each attempt is metered
with its attempt number, so the Health page's error rate still counts every failed call.

AI stays on in production: the cause is established. The practical remedy for the host
is operational, not code: a machine that runs the worker should not enter Modern Standby
during runs (Windows: *Settings → System → Power → Screen and sleep*, "When plugged in,
put my device to sleep after: Never"), or the worker should move to an always-on host.

**Item 2 — the failed classification on screen.** `_ai_summary` only ever read rows
*with output*, so an `error` row never reached the page. The review detail now also
carries `ai_attempt` — the newest `ai_classifications` row whatever its status — and
`AiSummaryBox` shows `error`, `schema_invalid` and `skipped_budget` attempts as "AI
classification failed — <status>, <time>" with the recorded error text, both when there
is no summary and when a failure is newer than the summary still shown. The "lead page"
in this spec is read as the review page (`/review/[businessId]`), the only page that
shows the AI summary; the approved-lead page (`/leads/[id]`) shows no AI summary at all,
so it has no "no summary" state to confuse.

**Item 3 — attribution, from Google's current documentation.** Sources fetched
2026-09-25: *Policies and attributions for Places API*
(developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/policies, last updated
2026-09-24), the Places API (New) `places` reference (2026-08-06), the Text Search (New)
page (for the SKU of `places.attributions`), the Google Maps Platform Terms of Service
(2026-08-26) and Service Specific Terms (2026-06-10). What they say, and what was built:

- *Wording.* "The following updated attribution requirements now use "Google Maps"
  instead of only "Google." … For new implementations, use "Google Maps"". "Powered by
  Google" appears on none of those pages. The spec's task line says "Powered by Google";
  the documentation governs, so the attribution is **Google Maps**. (The box is ticked for
  the attribution the task means, in the form the licence now requires.)
- *Form.* "Attribution should take the form of the Google Maps logo whenever possible."
  Google's own `Google_Maps_Attribution_Assets.zip` (linked from the policy page) is the
  source of `frontend/public/google-maps/GoogleMaps_Logo_Gray.svg`, byte-identical (a test
  pins its SHA-256). The non-outlined logo is for "a plain background"; the app is
  light-only, so the gray variant. Height 18 px (policy: 16–19), clear space 10 px left,
  right and top and 6 px below (policy: 10/10/10/5), `alt="Google Maps"` (policy: "an
  accessibility label with the text Google Maps"), `translate="no"`.
- *Placement.* "near the top or bottom of the content, and within the same visual
  container". Tables: a footer row inside the table's own bordered container, sticky to
  the left so horizontal scrolling cannot hide it. Cards: the bottom of the card holding
  the Places facts (review facts, the lead letterhead — so it prints too — each duplicate
  pair). The decision dialog carries its own under its business search results, since the
  dialog covers the page's.
- *Third-party providers.* Places returns `attributions[]`, "A set of data provider that
  must be shown with this result". The field mask did not request it, so no provider
  could ever have been shown. `places.attributions` is in the *Text Search Essentials ID
  Only* SKU — below the Pro/Enterprise fields already requested — so adding it does not
  change the cost of a call. Providers are read from the stored payload
  (`discovery/providers.py`) and shown beside the logo ("Data from …", linked only when
  the link is http(s)). On list pages, each page's distinct providers are shown in the
  footer. Places already fetched before this change have no `attributions` stored; they
  gain them on the next discovery that sees them. The decision dialog's business search
  (`/businesses`) does not carry providers: it shows the logo only.
- *Reviews/ratings.* Author attribution is required for photos and reviews; the product
  shows neither — only `rating` and `userRatingCount`. The only rating-specific guidance
  is marked "(recommended)". No review text or photo is requested (unchanged).

**Item 3 — the CRM export: decision.** No attribution field is added to the CRM record.
Reasons: (1) Google's attribution rules are written for display "in your app or website";
none of the pages fetched says anything about attribution inside exported data. (2) Every
exported record already carries its provenance — `source` ("Google Places") and
`source_url` (the place's Google Maps listing) — so a CRM user can always see where the
data came from. (3) A new field would need a new custom property created in the client's
CRM by their admin, and it would change every record's payload hash and trigger an
update of every synced record. (4) Most importantly, attribution is not the real question
for the export: the Terms of Service §3.2.3(a) say "Customer will not export, extract, or
otherwise scrape Google Maps Content for use outside the Services … (iii) copy and save
business names, addresses, or user reviews". Whether sending Places-derived fields to a
CRM is permitted at all is a legal question — the same one as the out-of-scope caching
question — and adding an attribution line would not answer it. Recorded under Blockers
for a human decision.

**`make e2e`.** Not run from this session: it targets `http://localhost:3000`, which is
the running *production* stack (`radar-prod-web-1` holds 127.0.0.1:3000 and :8000), and it
resets the demo review state before it starts. Running it would mean stopping production,
which is the human's call.
