# v0.11.1 — Safe to hand over

Status: draft
Branch: `spec/v0.11.1-safety-pass`
Previous: v0.11.0 (deeper audit), merged and tagged.

## Goal

v0.11.0 works. This spec is the short list of things that should not be in a build
somebody else runs.

Three items. None of them is a feature. Two are defects that lose data or hide failures
without saying so, and one is a licence obligation. Nothing here changes what the product
does — it makes what it already does trustworthy enough to hand to a client who will run
it without us watching.

Budget: about three days. If an item grows past that, it belongs in v0.12.0, not here.

## Scope

### 1. A failed audit must not hide a business for 30 days

`needs_audit` (`audit_web/service.py`) treats a business as fresh if it has *any* audit
inside `AUDIT_MAX_AGE_DAYS`, regardless of that audit's status. So one transient failure —
a timeout, a 5xx, a site that was briefly down — removes the business from every
subsequent run for a month, and nothing anywhere says so.

This is not theoretical. Two businesses are in that state right now, both from the RQ
timeout bug fixed in v0.11.0: **Hoffman Electric Company** and **Harlow Beauty and Hair
Salon**. Each carries a false `failed` audit as its latest, has zero opportunities, and
was skipped by the full overnight re-audit.

- A **failed** audit is due again after `AUDIT_FAILED_RETRY_HOURS` (default 6), not
  `AUDIT_MAX_AGE_DAYS`.
- A **successful** audit keeps the 30-day window unchanged.
- An **unreachable** site is a third case and needs its own decision: a site that is
  genuinely gone should not be retried every six hours forever. Propose a backoff, or a
  separate longer window, and say which you chose and why.
- Both existing damaged businesses become due immediately once this ships. Confirm they
  do, and that they then produce opportunities.

This is the highest-value item in the spec. Every lead it recovers is one the client paid
to discover and never saw.

### 2. Find out why one AI call in three fails

The Health page shows `openai: 35% (12 of 34 calls)` over 24 hours. All twelve were
`APIConnectionError: Connection error` — a connection failure, not auth, quota or rate
limiting. They began at 01:05:55 on 2026-09-25, seventy seconds before the host machine
slept, and continued for thirteen seconds after it woke.

The sleep is a plausible cause and is unconfirmed. It does not explain the seventy seconds
before it.

- Establish the cause. If it is the machine sleeping, prove it and record it — a laptop
  artefact is an acceptable answer, but only once it is demonstrated rather than assumed.
- If it is not the sleep, fix it.
- Either way, **a failed classification must be visible on the lead**. Today a business
  with no AI summary looks identical to one whose AI call failed. A reviewer cannot tell
  the difference, and neither could we.
- Add a retry with backoff for connection-class errors specifically, if the cause turns
  out to be transient. Do not retry auth or quota errors.

**If the cause cannot be established inside this spec's budget**, the fallback is to set
`AI_PROVIDER=disabled` in production and say so plainly. Shipping a feature that fails a
third of the time, silently, to somebody else's machine is worse than shipping without it.

### 3. "Powered by Google" attribution

Google's Maps Platform terms require attribution wherever Places data is displayed. The
product shows business names, phone numbers, addresses, ratings and review counts from
Places on the review queue, the lead detail page and the leads list, and exports them to
the CRM.

- Add the required attribution to every screen that displays Places-derived data.
- Follow Google's current attribution guidance for the wording and placement rather than
  inventing a form of words — check the documentation, do not reason from memory.
- Decide and record whether the CRM export also needs it. It carries Places data out of
  the product, which is the case the terms are concerned with.

This is a licence obligation on a product about to be run by a third party, not a polish
item.

## Out of scope

- **Audit concurrency.** Runs take 25–30 minutes for 50 businesses and that is slow, not
  broken. It is the largest remaining performance item and belongs in v0.12.0 with its own
  testing.
- Everything in `specs/v0.12.0.md`. This spec adds no findings, no checks, no columns.
- The Places billing tier verification, still blocked on the client's console access.
- Prompt `classify-2`, still deferred.
- The Maps caching question (place_id indefinitely, lat/lng 30 days), which needs legal
  input rather than code.

## Implementation notes

_(append only)_

**Why these three and nothing else.** This build is about to be run by somebody who cannot
read the logs, cannot diagnose a stuck run, and will not know that a business quietly
vanished. Each item here is a case where the product fails without telling anyone: a lead
disappears for a month, an AI summary is missing with no explanation, or a licence term is
breached invisibly. Features can wait. Silence cannot.
